# Wholehearted - Background, Purpose, Use and Privacy

> **Status:** Project background and user guidance for the Wholehearted W.H.E.M.S. prayer and discernment app.
>
> **Scope:** This document explains what the app is for, how to use it well, what to enter and not enter, how the human remains responsible for judgement and action, and what privacy means in practice.

## 1. What Wholehearted is

Wholehearted is a Christian prayer and discernment aid built around **W.H.E.M.S. - Will, Heart, Emotions, Mind and Soul**.

Its purpose is not merely to collect prayer requests. It is meant to help a person bring a real situation before God, understand what is actually happening, examine the matter through Scripture and W.H.E.M.S., distinguish gratitude from struggle, consider a faithful next step, pray in several forms, and preserve the journey for later reflection and thanksgiving.

A typical concern may be simple or complex:

- “Should I speak now, wait, or first listen?”
- “Am I reacting from fear, anger, pride, responsibility, compassion, or incomplete information?”
- “Should I act, pray, seek counsel, or do several of these together?”
- “What has God already helped me with, and where am I still struggling?”
- “What does Scripture make clear, and what remains a matter of wisdom?”

Wholehearted is designed to slow down impulsive certainty without turning prayer into indecision. It helps the user move from an unstructured concern towards a more truthful, prayerful and responsible response.

## 2. The basic biblical framing

W.H.E.M.S. is a **prayer scaffold**, not a biblical five-part anatomy of the human person.

The categories are used because Scripture speaks richly about willing, loving, desiring, feeling, thinking, belonging, obeying, trusting and serving. The categories overlap. They are not five sealed compartments, and a single biblical word can touch more than one category.

The source teaching used in this project states the basic questions simply:

- **W - Will:** What do I want?
- **H - Heart:** What is ruling within me?
- **E - Emotions:** What am I feeling?
- **M - Mind:** What is true?
- **S - Soul:** Whose am I?

The source also supplies a simple prayer pattern:

### During conflict

- **W:** Lord, what do I want, and does it honour You?
- **H:** What is ruling my heart beneath this disagreement?
- **E:** Here is what I feel. Keep these feelings from ruling me.
- **M:** Show me what is true, what Scripture says, and where my assumptions may be wrong.
- **S:** I belong to Christ. Let me respond as one saved by grace rather than as a slave to pride, fear or sin.

### Afterwards - thanksgiving

- **W:** Thank You for directing me.
- **H:** Thank You for shaping me.
- **E:** Thank You for sustaining me.
- **M:** Thank You for teaching me.
- **S:** Thank You that I am Yours by grace.

The project also retains the following teaching controls:

- W.H.E.M.S. serves Scripture. It does not control Scripture.
- Soul as “Saved or Slave to Sin” is an allegiance check, not a licence to pronounce another person's hidden spiritual state.
- Emotion is real but not sovereign.
- Mind is not cold rationalism.
- Thanksgiving is more than relief after circumstances improve.

The one-line summary is:

> In conflict, bring your **Will, Heart, Emotions, Mind and Soul** before God; after His direction becomes clearer, bring the same five areas back to Him in thanksgiving.

## 3. What Wholehearted is not

Wholehearted is **not**:

- a message from God;
- prophecy;
- a substitute for Scripture;
- a substitute for prayer itself;
- a substitute for a pastor, mature Christian counsel, family responsibility or professional help;
- a salvation test;
- a spiritual score;
- an answer-rate tracker;
- a system for declaring that a strong feeling is God’s guidance;
- a system for discovering hidden motives in another person;
- a system for turning every disagreement into a sin diagnosis;
- a majority vote where three “positive” W.H.E.M.S. areas automatically defeat two concerns;
- a guarantee that prayer will remove uncertainty;
- a guarantee that a chosen next step will succeed;
- a guarantee that the longest prayer is the best prayer.

The app can organise thought and prayer. The human user remains responsible for judgement, repentance, obedience, seeking counsel, respecting others, and taking or withholding action.

## 4. The human framing: how to approach the app

The healthiest posture is:

> **“I am bringing a matter before God. I may be partly right, partly mistaken, emotionally affected, lacking information, or mixing several motives. I want Scripture to govern me, truth to correct me, love to shape me, and Christ to be honoured in my response.”**

Use the app as a **discernment companion**, not as an oracle.

### Bring a question, not a verdict

Better:

> “My daughter is finding the first stage of her research year difficult. I am wondering whether to call her, ask whether she wants to talk, give advice, or simply pray and wait.”

Less helpful:

> “God is telling me that I must intervene and she is refusing my God-given authority.”

The second wording has already converted interpretation into certainty. Wholehearted works best when the situation is described before conclusions are imposed.

### Separate what happened from what you think it means

Better:

> “She said the workload feels steep and that she is still learning the research process.”

Then separately:

> “I worry that she may be discouraged, but I do not know whether that is how she sees it.”

This distinction protects both truth and relationship.

### Prayer and responsible action are not rivals

Do not force the question into:

> “Should I pray or should I act?”

Often the better question is:

> “What responsible action, if any, belongs to me while I continue praying?”

### Do not treat peace or discomfort as automatic verdicts

A peaceful feeling can accompany a wise or unwise decision. Anxiety can accompany obedience as well as folly. Emotions matter, but they require interpretation under Scripture, truth, responsibility and wise counsel.

## 5. The Wholehearted flow

The intended flow is:

**Describe → clarify → reflect through W.H.E.M.S. → consider Scripture and options → choose a next faithful step → pray → save or return later in thanksgiving.**

The app should help the user at each stage without silently taking ownership of the decision.

## 6. Step 1 - Describe the issue

The main field asks:

> **What is happening, and what are you trying to decide?**

Write enough for the real question to be understandable.

Good content includes:

- the situation;
- what decision or response is being considered;
- relevant people described by relationship rather than unnecessary identity;
- what is known;
- what is uncertain;
- what action, restraint or conversation is being considered.

A useful structure is:

> “I am considering **[decision/action]** because **[situation]**. I know **[facts]**. I am uncertain about **[unknowns]**. I want to respond in a way that honours Christ and is responsible towards **[people affected]**.”

### What not to put in the issue field

Avoid unnecessary:

- full names;
- NRIC, passport or identity numbers;
- home addresses;
- telephone numbers;
- personal email addresses;
- passwords, access tokens or API keys;
- medical record numbers;
- bank or card information;
- private employer identifiers that are not needed;
- another person's confidential history;
- copied private messages when a short paraphrase is enough.

If the app can reason about the situation without identifying the person, prefer:

> “my adult daughter”, “my colleague”, “a church member”, “my supervisor”, “a family member”.

## 7. Step 2 - Add context or correction

The context field is for facts that materially change interpretation.

Useful context may include:

- what another person actually said;
- whether advice was requested;
- whether someone asked for space;
- whether a responsibility is yours or belongs to another person;
- time constraints;
- previous attempts;
- facts that would make one option more or less appropriate.

Example:

> “She is an adult and has welcomed contact. She has not asked me to solve the research problem. I do not yet know whether she wants advice or simply someone to listen.”

This is more useful than adding large amounts of biography.

## 8. Optional contextual AI guidance

Contextual guidance is optional.

Without AI, the user can still use the open W.H.E.M.S. prompts and create local structured prayer drafts.

When AI guidance is deliberately requested and consented to, the app may send the **current issue, current context, relevant replies and selected notes** to the configured OpenAI model so it can propose:

- a summary of the concern;
- facts explicitly stated by the user;
- important uncertainties;
- situation-specific W.H.E.M.S. questions;
- conditional options;
- draft prayer forms.

The AI is instructed not to:

- infer hidden motives as facts;
- declare a person's salvation;
- claim to know God's private will;
- invent feelings another person has not expressed;
- turn distress into a sin diagnosis;
- turn a strong emotion into authority;
- make a more expensive model substitution automatically;
- treat a generated prayer as historical or inspired wording.

## 9. Human acknowledgement of AI understanding

When AI guidance proposes a summary, read it as:

> **“Is this a fair description of what I meant?”**

It is not a required confession and not a verdict.

Correct it when:

- it assumes a motive you did not express;
- it treats an inference as a fact;
- it misunderstands who has responsibility;
- it overlooks something material;
- it changes “I am wondering whether…” into “I should…”;
- it treats another person's feelings or intentions as known when they are not.

Only acknowledge the summary when it fairly represents your concern.

The app should never tick this acknowledgement automatically.

## 10. W - Will

### Core question

> **What do I want?**

Will concerns intended action, desired outcome, willingness, resistance and obedience.

Useful things to write:

- what you want to do;
- what you hope will happen;
- what you are reluctant to do;
- whether you are willing to change course;
- whether you are trying to force an outcome beyond your responsibility.

Examples:

> “I want to help, but I am unsure whether advice would actually help.”

> “I want the conflict resolved quickly, and I recognise that this may make me impatient.”

Do not write a confession merely because the app asks about will. If you do not know whether a desire is sinful, describe it honestly first.

## 11. H - Heart

### Core question

> **What is ruling within me?**

Heart concerns loves, loyalties, motives, desires and what is being treated as most important.

Useful things to write:

- what good you are seeking;
- what you fear losing;
- what outcome feels emotionally necessary;
- what motive you recognise;
- where you want God to search and correct you.

Better:

> “I want to support her, but I also notice that I feel responsible to make the problem go away.”

Not better:

> “I am obviously controlling.”

unless that is something you genuinely recognise and want to confess.

The app must not convert a possible motive into a fact.

## 12. E - Emotions

### Core question

> **What am I feeling?**

Emotions are real information about the person's experience. They are not automatically commands, sins or divine signals.

Useful things to write:

- concerned;
- angry;
- hurt;
- afraid;
- relieved;
- grateful;
- disappointed;
- hopeful;
- confused;
- emotionally numb;
- mixed feelings.

Where another person is involved, distinguish:

- **what I feel**;
- **what the other person explicitly said they feel**;
- **what I suspect but do not know**.

Example:

> “I feel concerned and somewhat helpless. She has said the learning curve is steep. I do not know whether she feels discouraged.”

## 13. M - Mind

### Core question

> **What is true?**

Mind concerns facts, Scripture, assumptions, interpretation, consequences, evidence and wise counsel.

Useful things to write:

- what is directly known;
- what is inferred;
- what Scripture clearly commands or forbids;
- what Scripture teaches as principle;
- what remains a wisdom judgement;
- what information is missing;
- what counsel may be needed.

Ask:

- What do I actually know?
- What am I assuming?
- What would change my view?
- Have I heard the other person fairly?
- Am I using one verse without its context?
- Is this a direct command or an application of wisdom?

## 14. S - Soul

### Core question

> **Whose am I?**

Within this project, Soul carries the concern of **Saved or Slave to Sin** as an allegiance question.

For a Christian, this asks how belonging to Christ should shape the decision and response.

It does **not** mean:

- every bad decision proves a person is unsaved;
- disagreement with another Christian proves slavery to sin;
- anxiety, grief or confusion proves unbelief;
- the app can determine another person's standing before God.

Useful reflections may include:

> “Because I belong to Christ, I do not need to secure every outcome myself.”

> “I want to act as one saved by grace, not as one driven by pride or fear.”

> “I am still exploring faith in Christ and do not want the prayer to pretend a certainty I have not expressed.”

The app should respect the user's own stated relationship to Christ and must not fabricate conversion language.

## 15. Thanksgiving and request can coexist

Each W.H.E.M.S. area may contain:

- thanksgiving;
- request for help;
- both;
- neither yet.

This is important.

Example:

> “Father, thank You that You have made me willing to listen. I am still afraid of saying the wrong thing. Please help me speak gently and not make my fear the centre of the conversation.”

A person does not need five “positive” areas before acting. Neither do two areas of concern automatically veto an action.

The purpose is not arithmetic. It is truthful prayer and discernment.

## 16. Discernment notes

Use discernment notes to record what seems significant after considering Scripture, facts, responsibilities and W.H.E.M.S.

Good notes distinguish:

- what Scripture makes clear;
- what is inferred;
- what remains unknown;
- what another person has actually asked for;
- what responsibility belongs to the user;
- what may require counsel.

Example:

> “Prayer and contact are not opposites. Because she has welcomed contact but has not asked for advice, a proportionate next step may be to ask whether she would like to talk and begin by listening.”

This is a wisdom conclusion, not a declaration that God has privately revealed the outcome.

## 17. The next faithful step

The next-step field is for an action the **human user chooses**.

Examples:

- “Ask whether she would like to talk this weekend.”
- “Listen first and ask before giving advice.”
- “Wait 24 hours before replying to the message.”
- “Speak with a pastor before confronting the person.”
- “Gather the missing financial information before deciding.”
- “Do not act on the rumour until it is verified.”
- “Pray, apologise for the words I actually spoke, and ask for a conversation.”

A good next step is often smaller than the desired final outcome.

## 18. Three prayer forms

Wholehearted presents the same matter in three forms.

### One-sentence prayer

Purpose:

- remember the issue during the day;
- begin when words are difficult;
- keep the central thanksgiving and request clear.

Longer is not more spiritual.

### W.H.E.M.S. prayer

Purpose:

- pray through Will, Heart, Emotions, Mind and Soul explicitly;
- preserve mixed thanksgiving and requests;
- reflect the user's actual answers rather than generic spiritual language.

### Extended prayer

Purpose:

- provide an original, reverent, Christ-centred, Puritan-influenced prayer;
- move through adoration, approach through Christ, dependence on the Holy Spirit, thanksgiving, self-examination, petition, intercession, faithful action and entrusting the outcome.

The extended prayer is **new wording**, not a quotation from *The Valley of Vision* or another historical Christian writer.

Review and edit all three before using or sharing them.

## 19. What to do when a prayer draft feels wrong

Do not force yourself to accept generated wording.

Edit or remove wording when it:

- says you feel something you do not feel;
- confesses a sin you have not recognised;
- describes another person's motive as known;
- implies God guaranteed a result;
- sounds spiritually impressive but is not truthful;
- overstates certainty;
- is too public for the people named;
- uses a theological phrase you do not understand.

A truthful short prayer is better than an eloquent false one.

## 20. What “guidance” means in this app

Guidance means **help with framing, questions, distinctions and possible next steps**.

It does not mean the AI is spiritually guiding the user as the Holy Spirit does.

A useful AI response should:

- restate the issue accurately;
- distinguish fact from inference;
- expose missing information without inventing it;
- ask context-sensitive W.H.E.M.S. questions;
- change its advice when the user's facts change;
- avoid accusing the user of motives not admitted;
- avoid interpreting another person's heart;
- suggest proportionate options;
- leave the decision with the human user.

## 21. Scripture in Wholehearted

Scripture is authoritative. Editorial guidance and AI-generated application are not.

The app's Scripture reader exists so a user can check passages without losing the writing flow.

When reading a passage, distinguish:

1. **Scripture text** - what the biblical text actually says.
2. **Context** - what the passage is doing in its literary and historical setting.
3. **Application** - how the passage may bear on the user's present concern.

Do not collapse these into one statement.

The app should not present an AI paraphrase as though it were ESV wording.

## 22. Best way to use Wholehearted

A productive use pattern is:

1. **Write the issue plainly.**
2. **Remove unnecessary identifying information.**
3. **State what is known and unknown.**
4. **Use contextual guidance only if useful and only after understanding the privacy boundary.**
5. **Correct the proposed understanding.**
6. **Work through all five W.H.E.M.S. areas, but do not force an answer where you do not yet have one.**
7. **Mark thanksgiving and requests independently.**
8. **Read the relevant Scripture in context.**
9. **Record what remains uncertain.**
10. **Choose a proportionate next faithful step.**
11. **Review and edit all three prayers.**
12. **Save deliberately if you want a journal record.**
13. **Return later and record thanksgiving, changed circumstances, a different answer, or unresolved questions.**

## 23. When not to use the app alone

Do not make the app the sole source of guidance when the matter involves:

- immediate danger;
- self-harm or harm to another person;
- abuse;
- coercion;
- serious medical symptoms;
- legal deadlines;
- large or irreversible financial consequences;
- safeguarding concerns involving children or vulnerable adults;
- a situation requiring emergency services;
- a matter where competent professional advice is necessary.

Prayer remains appropriate, but prayer does not remove ordinary responsibilities to obtain help.

## 24. Privacy - the short version

Wholehearted has **different privacy boundaries for different features**.

The journal is designed to be local to the browser. AI guidance is not local because selected current content must be sent to an external model provider when the user explicitly chooses AI guidance.

Therefore:

> **Local journal does not mean every feature is offline or private from external service providers.**

## 25. Privacy - local journal storage

Saved journal entries are stored in the browser's local storage on that device/browser profile.

Practical meaning:

- there is no Wholehearted cloud journal account;
- another browser or device will not automatically contain the same entries;
- clearing browser/site data can remove the saved journal;
- someone with access to the same unlocked browser profile may be able to access the journal;
- browser local storage is not the same thing as end-to-end encryption;
- private browsing/incognito behaviour may not preserve entries reliably;
- device backup, enterprise management or browser synchronisation may have effects outside the app's control.

Use device-level security if the journal contains sensitive material.

## 26. Privacy - AI guidance

AI guidance is opt-in.

When the user deliberately chooses AI guidance after consent, the app sends the **current material needed for that request** to the configured OpenAI service.

The design intent is to send current fields such as:

- issue;
- context;
- current W.H.E.M.S. replies;
- relevant questions;
- selected thanksgiving/request notes;
- next step where applicable.

The app is designed not to send:

- the entire journal;
- unrelated old entries;
- hidden revision history;
- API keys;
- deselected private notes merely because they exist locally.

The server requests `store:false`, but that is **not a promise of zero provider retention**. OpenAI's applicable service and retention policies still govern provider-side processing.

Therefore, before sending a concern to AI:

- minimise identifying details;
- paraphrase another person's private information;
- do not paste secrets;
- do not assume that “local journal” means the AI request is local.

## 27. Privacy - Scripture reader

The app may use Crossway's official ESV reader or API.

When a built-in passage is opened, the external Scripture service receives the **public passage reference** and normal browser/network metadata required to serve the content.

The design intent is not to send the user's prayer or journal text to Crossway.

Scripture retrieval and journal storage are separate data paths.

## 28. Privacy - exports and downloads

HTML, text and JSON exports become ordinary files once downloaded.

After download, Wholehearted no longer controls:

- where the file is stored;
- whether it is copied to cloud storage;
- whether it is included in backups;
- whether it is forwarded;
- whether another person can open it.

JSON backups are especially sensitive because they preserve structured journal data.

Treat them like a private journal archive.

## 29. Privacy - shared or congregational prayer

Wholehearted can prepare personal or “we/our” prayer language.

Changing “I” to “we” does not automatically make private details suitable for public prayer.

Before sharing:

- remove names unless needed and appropriate;
- remove confidential medical, family, employment or counselling details;
- avoid narrating another person's sins, motives or struggles without permission;
- generalise third-party information;
- read the whole prayer aloud privately before using it publicly.

## 30. What not to enter anywhere

Never enter:

- passwords;
- API keys;
- authentication codes;
- banking PINs;
- full card details;
- identity-document numbers;
- confidential security answers;
- secrets belonging to another person;
- material you are not entitled to disclose.

For highly sensitive pastoral, medical, legal, family or workplace matters, prefer a concise description that preserves the reasoning problem without exposing unnecessary identity.

## 31. Journal philosophy

The Journal is meant to preserve prayer and discernment, not create pressure to perform spiritually.

It should therefore:

- open with a fresh writing space rather than exposing the last request;
- show old entries only when the user deliberately browses;
- avoid prayer-excerpt feeds on the landing view;
- preserve original wording and later revisions;
- distinguish prayer date, creation time and updated time;
- allow Request, Thanksgiving and Mixed entries;
- allow linked follow-ups;
- allow the user to record answer status rather than infer it.

## 32. Answer status is human-entered

A thanksgiving entry does not automatically mean the original request was answered as requested.

Possible realities include:

- waiting;
- partly answered;
- answered as requested;
- answered differently;
- direction became clearer;
- circumstances changed;
- still unresolved;
- closed or no longer applicable.

The user records this. The app should not infer it from sentiment or from prayer language.

## 33. Revisiting a prayer

When returning later, ask:

- What changed in the situation?
- What changed in me?
- What did not change?
- What did I learn?
- Was my earlier assumption correct?
- Did another person ask for something different?
- What thanksgiving is now appropriate?
- What request remains?
- Do I need to apologise, act, wait, seek counsel, or accept an outcome I cannot control?

A later entry should preserve history rather than rewrite the original concern into a cleaner story.

## 34. Failure messages and incomplete AI output

A technical AI failure is not a spiritual judgement.

The app should distinguish, where possible:

- missing human input;
- missing consent;
- unconfirmed understanding;
- provider authentication/configuration problem;
- provider rate limit;
- connection or timeout;
- incomplete response;
- malformed response;
- an incomplete individual prayer form.

If one prayer form is incomplete, usable returned forms should not be discarded.

The user should be able to:

- keep existing wording;
- retry only the missing form;
- use a local draft;
- write personally without AI.

## 35. Theological guardrails for generated prayers

Generated prayers should not:

- claim God promised an outcome Scripture does not promise;
- claim a user has confessed a sin they did not identify;
- claim another person's hidden motive;
- treat suffering as proof of weak faith;
- treat success as proof of divine approval;
- use salvation language the user has not affirmed;
- call a wisdom judgement a command from God;
- imply the AI has spiritual authority;
- falsely attribute new wording to Augustine, Anselm, Matthew Henry, Puritan writers or *The Valley of Vision*.

Generated prayers may ask God to search, correct and reveal what is actually present without inventing what that must be.

## 36. A note for new believers

You do not need to understand all five categories before beginning.

Start with one honest sentence:

> “Father, this is what is happening, this is what I want, and I need Your help to respond faithfully.”

Then use W.H.E.M.S. slowly.

The point is not to become skilled at the acronym. The point is to become more truthful before God, more attentive to Scripture, more willing to be corrected, more loving towards people, and more faithful in response.

## 37. A note for mature believers and leaders

Do not use W.H.E.M.S. to interrogate people or make them confess to categories chosen by the facilitator.

Use it to help a person distinguish:

- desire from command;
- motive from accusation;
- feeling from authority;
- fact from assumption;
- allegiance to Christ from performance.

Where Scripture is clear, do not hide disobedience behind endless reflection. Where Scripture leaves room for wisdom, do not manufacture certainty.

## 38. What good use of the app should produce

The best outcome is not merely a polished prayer.

A good session should leave the user better able to say:

- “This is the actual issue.”
- “These are the facts I know.”
- “These are my assumptions.”
- “This is what I want.”
- “This is what I am feeling.”
- “This is what Scripture makes clear.”
- “This is what remains uncertain.”
- “This responsibility belongs to me.”
- “This outcome does not belong to me to control.”
- “This is my next faithful step.”
- “This is what I can thank God for now.”
- “This is where I still need help.”

## 39. Example - a father supporting an adult daughter

### Issue

> “My adult daughter has begun a demanding research-study year. She says the learning curve is steep. I am wondering whether I should call and advise her, offer to listen, or simply pray and give her space.”

### Helpful context

> “She has welcomed contact, but she has not asked me to solve the research problem. I do not know yet whether she wants advice.”

### W - Will

> “I want to help and reduce her difficulty. I am willing to listen first if that is more helpful than giving advice.”

### H - Heart

> “I want her good. I also notice that I feel responsible to make the problem easier, and I want God to search whether that is becoming controlling.”

### E - Emotions

> “I feel concerned and somewhat helpless. She has said the learning curve is steep. I should not assume she feels defeated.”

### M - Mind

> “I know she is learning a new research process. I do not yet know what specific part is hardest or what help she has requested. I should understand before advising.”

### S - Soul

> “Because I belong to Christ, I can care faithfully without treating myself as the one who must secure the outcome.”

### Possible next step

> “Continue praying and ask whether a listening conversation would help. Begin by listening, and ask before offering advice.”

This is a wisdom application based on the stated context. It is not a private revelation from God and should change if the facts change.

## 40. Frugality and restraint in use

AI calls cost money and transmit selected text externally.

Therefore:

- use local W.H.E.M.S. when the issue is already clear;
- use contextual AI when adaptive questioning materially helps;
- do not regenerate repeatedly just to obtain more elegant wording;
- edit a good draft rather than treating variation as improvement;
- use Scripture and human counsel instead of escalating model complexity automatically;
- prefer the configured model rather than hidden expensive fallback.

## 41. Technical privacy boundaries for maintainers

The intended technical invariants are:

- journal storage remains local unless a future cloud feature is explicitly designed and consented to;
- AI requests are explicit, not triggered by typing;
- whole journal history is not sent to AI by default;
- deselected notes are not sent merely because they exist locally;
- secrets remain server-side;
- model choice is explicit;
- no silent model upgrade;
- Scripture requests use allowlisted public references;
- technical error logging avoids prayer text, raw model output and credentials;
- exports are escaped so private text cannot become executable HTML;
- a failed save must not be reported as successful;
- cross-tab changes should not be silently overwritten;
- old journal formats should not be destructively migrated without an explicit save.

## 42. Product principle

Wholehearted should always make the distinction visible:

> **Scripture is authoritative. The user's words are personal testimony. AI guidance is a proposed interpretation. A prayer draft is editable assistance. The decision and obedience remain human responsibilities before God.**

That distinction is central to both discipleship and safety.

## 43. Closing perspective

Wholehearted is most useful when it helps a person become less performative, less impulsive and less dependent on artificial certainty.

It should encourage:

- honesty before God;
- careful attention to Scripture;
- humility about motives and missing facts;
- love for Christ;
- patience towards others;
- willingness to repent when correction is warranted;
- willingness to act when responsibility is clear;
- restraint when another person's agency should be respected;
- thanksgiving for what God has already shaped;
- continued prayer where the matter remains unresolved.

The app is successful when it helps the user pray more truthfully and respond more faithfully - not when it appears to make the decision for them.

## 44. Source note

The project's W.H.E.M.S. teaching pattern and its Acts 15 guardrails were developed from the project's Bible-study material, including the source that explicitly presents W.H.E.M.S. as a prayer scaffold rather than a five-part biblical anthropology. The app extends that prayer scaffold into a guided discernment and journal workflow while preserving Scripture as the authority.

For implementation details, runtime configuration and current technical constraints, also read the repository `README.md` and the source code/tests for the build being reviewed.
